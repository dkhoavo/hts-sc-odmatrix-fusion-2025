# Uncovering latent urban mobility patterns via smart-card and survey data fusion

Multiday, multimodal, time-dependent origin–destination (TD–OD) flows provide a detailed representation of when, where, and how urban travel occurs. However, most existing TD–OD flow estimation/forecasting approaches remain single-mode or mode-agnostic and depend on dense multimodal observations that are rarely available at scale, limiting their applicability for system-level analysis and policy evaluation. A city-scale data-fusion framework is presented for recovering multiday, multimodal TD–OD flows under partial observability. Household travel surveys, which provide sparse but mode-complete information on cross-modal composition, are integrated with smart-card transactions that offer near-census, high-resolution transit TD–OD observations across days and times. By explicitly separating slowly-varying (stable) cross-modal mode-share ratios from fast-varying demand magnitude and temporal allocation, survey-derived mode-share relationships are generalized and anchored to contemporaneous transit flows. This formulation enables recovery of private-vehicle and walking flows at hourly resolution across weekday groups, without requiring direct passive observations for all modes or day-specific survey calibration. The recovered TD–OD matrices are validated against independent passive mobility datasets at the city scale in Singapore and Seoul, covering more than two million OD–time records. OD–time–level Common Part of Commuters (CPC) values exceeding 0.71 and $R^2$ values above 0.62 are obtained, indicating strong consistency in both spatial structure and temporal dynamics. The resulting multimodal TD–OD matrices support several policy-relevant analyses. Distance-based benchmarking reveals a robust U-shaped pattern of transit competitiveness, with private modes dominating very short and long trips and transit performing best at intermediate distances (approximately 11–16 km). When integrated into epidemic simulations, reliance on transit-only mobility data is shown to underestimate epidemic peaks by up to 50\%. Taken together, a general and modular framework for multimodal TD–OD recovery is established, enabling city-scale mobility analysis in data-scarce settings and strengthening the empirical basis for transport planning and public health preparedness.
